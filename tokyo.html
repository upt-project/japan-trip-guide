Tokyo in one day

Tokyo is too big for one day - unless the day is one clean arc. This route runs from old Tokyo in the east to the neon west, costs ¥963 in train fares, and every stop on it is free to enter.

The route: east to west, no doubling back

TimeStop
8:00-10:45Asakusa - Senso-ji temple and the Nakamise shopping street, before the crowds peak
11:00-11:45Lunch in Asakusa - tendon at Daikokuya (¥2,300-¥2,700, cash only)
12:05-13:00Ueno Park and the Ameyoko market streets
13:30-14:45Imperial Palace East Gardens (free)
15:05-16:15Ginza - flagship stores and department-store food halls
16:45-17:45Shibuya - the scramble crossing at its busiest
18:15-19:15Shinjuku - the free observatory in the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building

The trains cost ¥963 all day, and admissions cost ¥0. With lunch the day totals roughly ¥3,263-¥3,663. No day pass pays off on this route - a Welcome Suica or a phone IC card is all you need. Meiji Jingu and Shinjuku Gyoen are left for day two on purpose; Akihabara is the easy swap if it matters more to you than Ueno.

Arriving from Narita Airport

The guide's arrival section compares the two fast trains: the Narita Express (¥3,140, about 53 minutes to Tokyo Station) and the Keisei Skyliner (¥2,580, about 36-41 minutes to Ueno - handy because this route starts in the east of the city).
