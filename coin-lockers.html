Coin lockers in Japan: sizes, prices and the rules that catch people out

Almost every station in Japan has coin lockers, and they are the cheapest way to sightsee without your suitcase. Three rules decide what you actually pay: the price is per day, a new day starts at 2 a.m. for many operators, and most lockers keep a bag for three days at most.

Sizes and prices

Prices are shown on each locker and vary by station and operator, but the pattern is the same everywhere. JR East's published typical prices give the shape of it:

SizeFitsPrice per day
SmallA daypack or shopping bagsAbout ¥300-¥500
MediumA cabin-size suitcaseAbout ¥500-¥800
LargeA full-size checked suitcaseAbout ¥900-¥1,000

At JR Osaka Station, one bank of lockers charges ¥400 / ¥500 / ¥700 for small, medium and large - the point is not the exact number but the range: budget roughly ¥1,000 a day for a big case, and check the price on the locker door before you close it.

The 2 a.m. rule

A locker "day" is not 24 hours from when you pay. Many operators - including Tokyo Metro, Tokyu and JR East's Multi eCube lockers - start a new day at 2:00 in the morning. Leave a bag at 8 p.m. and collect it at 9 the next morning, and you may be charged for two days. The extra charge is collected when you open the locker, so keep coins or your IC card handy at pickup.

The 3-day limit

Most station lockers allow up to 3 days of storage, counting the first day (Tokyo Metro allows 4). After the limit, staff remove the bag to a storage office; you can still claim it - usually for up to a month - but storage fees keep accruing and you will need ID and the locker receipt. A locker is for a day of sightseeing or a short side trip, not for abandoning your main suitcase for a week.

Paying: coins, IC card, or neither

  • Older lockers take 100-yen coins only - carry a few.
  • Many lockers are locked and opened with a transport IC card (Suica, Pasmo, Icoca and the rest): tap to lock, tap the same card to open. The card is your key, so do not lose track of which card you used.
  • Newer touchscreen lockers (such as JR East's Multi eCube) also take QR payments and credit cards, and print a receipt or PIN instead.
  • Whatever the type, photograph the locker number and keep the receipt or PIN.

When the large lockers are full

Large lockers are the first to go, especially at big stations on weekends. Arrive early, try the locker banks on other floors or near a different exit, or use a staffed luggage counter: at JR Osaka Station, Crosta Osaka takes bags for ¥1,000 per item per day (card only, 9:00-20:00, open 365 days). Similar counters exist at major stations and airports, and they take the oversized bags lockers cannot.
