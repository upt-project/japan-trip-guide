What is otoshi? The izakaya charge that is not a scam

You sit down in an izakaya, order a beer - and before anything you asked for arrives, a small dish is put in front of you. You didn't order it. At the end of the night it is on your bill. That dish is called otoshi, and understanding it takes about two minutes.

Otoshi, in short

Otoshi is a small appetiser served automatically when you sit down at most izakaya, and it works as the pub's cover charge. At a casual izakaya it is usually ¥300-¥500 per person; most places fall somewhere in the ¥300-¥800 range, and a high-end place can reach about ¥1,000. Japanese customers pay it too - it is not a tourist charge, and it is not a mistake on your bill.

The catch is that it is charged per person and rarely appears on the menu board outside. The moment to ask is before you sit down, not when the bill arrives.

The three charges people mix up

ChargeWhat it isTypical amount
Otoshi (お通し)The automatic small dish at izakaya; doubles as the cover charge¥300-¥500 per person
Seating charge (席料)A per-person seat fee, common at bars and some restaurants - no dish included¥300-¥1,000 per person
Service chargeA percentage added at hotels and upscale restaurants10-15% of the bill

Two more lines appear on real bills: a late-night surcharge (often 10% after 22:00) at some places, and consumption tax - 10% for eating in, already included in menu prices by law. Tipping is not customary in Japan.

Can you refuse otoshi?

No. Otoshi works as a seating charge: once you sit down, it is part of the bill, whether you eat the dish or not. What you can do is check before you sit down - look for お通し on the menu by the door and ask how much it is. If you don't like the answer, that is the moment to choose another shop.
